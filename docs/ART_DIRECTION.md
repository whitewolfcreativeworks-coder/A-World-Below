# Visual benchmark: the Vent Garden

The central hook is the search for extremophiles and other unusual organisms,
studying their adaptations, and developing research-inspired village technology.
Detailed 3D examination is a core interaction. Visual quality takes priority over
expanding the generated world or adding more collection systems.

## Current visual study

One compact authored vent habitat sits within the seeded terrain. Only nine
terrain chunks load around the player. The study includes textured craggy rock,
original ridged chimney geometry, particulate plumes, expedition lighting,
depth-based color absorption, and an original Blender-built Riftia illustration.
The biological illustration has 20,056 imported vertices across its tube,
interior, collar, plume, and fine-filament surfaces. Its Blender source and
reproducible generator are included, alongside commercial-use-compatible CC0
bare-rock maps and their provenance.

The inspector provides an independent, lit 3D world, full orbit, pinch/scroll and
button zoom, and anatomy-focused views. This is not the final anatomical model.
Proportions and fine structure are interpreted rather than measured. The earlier
jelly, octopus, capsule, and isopod forms remain placeholders.

## Quality gate before world expansion

- The habitat must read as water and rock with convincing scale, depth, and
  deliberate lighting. Materials need to hold up at swimming distance.
- A hero organism must remain convincing when rotated and examined close up.
  Focus on structure, motion, tissue, and reference accuracy rather than polygon
  counts alone. Revise or replace the illustration if it does not meet that bar.
- Make discovery useful: observation leads to understanding an adaptation,
  evidence collection, an experiment, and a useful village upgrade.
- Validate touch controls, visibility, and sustained performance on the owner's
  actual iPad. Target a stable 30 fps or better, with a quality setting if needed.
  This target has not yet been measured on a physical iPad.

Godot's browser-compatible renderer supports this study. Desktop Forward+
volumetrics and other expensive rendering techniques are a separate future
option, not assumed to work in the iPad browser build. Free software provides
production tools; compelling anatomy, animation, and art direction still need
iterative work and reference checking.

## Research progression direction

Proposed loop: discover → examine → sample/observe → experiment → invent → build.
The current specimen view explains the loop but does not yet award research or
unlock a functioning bioreactor.

Riftia's bacterial symbiosis offers the first chain: understand the partnership,
then investigate a fictional microbial culture/bioreactor module. Chemosynthesis
is not free oxygen production. Later chains could explore thermostable enzymes,
pressure adaptation, biomaterials, and biological light/reporters, clearly
distinguishing observed adaptations from fictional engineering extrapolations.

Microbes require a future microscopy/schematic layer with honest scale and
provenance. A close-up animal model is not a substitute for microbial detail.
