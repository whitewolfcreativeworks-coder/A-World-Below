A World Below / Research & world design / 11 November 2026

Different worlds.
One continuous ocean.

A collection of real seabed photographs and nine habitat recipes. Build each region like an aquarium: terrain first, then water conditions, communities, movement and a discovery worth making.

These are scientific reference images and proposed designs, not screenshots of new game environments. The existing game still contains the vent-garden prototype. Distances, encounter frequency and regional proximity will be compressed for play.

A journey through the mosaic

Select a habitat to examine its photo and recipe. This is a schematic design sketch, not a geographic map or the running game generator.

1 / Read the terrain

Ridges, walls, depressions and sediment fields establish places. Randomness varies the forms inside a coherent region.

2 / Follow the conditions

Depth, substrate, food, current and chemical sources determine which communities can live there.

3 / Discover the hotspot

Scattered clues lead to a richer colony or rare encounter. Quieter stretches still contain tracks, burrows and small discoveries.

The deep ocean is not a sunlit planted aquarium. Corals and sponges are animals; most true plants need light. Microbial communities support some dark-ocean food webs. ROV lamps reveal colors that would otherwise be hidden in darkness.

01 / Current-fed outcrop

Coral terraces

Branching fans rise above rough stone. Small animals occupy branches, ledges and crevices; bare rock remains visible between colonies.

Cold-water coralsBrittle starsSquat lobstersAttached sponges

Reference: Papahānaumokuākea expedition coral-and-sponge community; exact image depth not verified.

Explore the habitat recipe

Terrain to build

Layered hard outcrops with sheltered pockets and exposed crests.

Movement and atmosphere

Flexible branches respond gently to current; drifting food particles show flow direction.

Arrival

A few isolated fans appear first. Around a ridge, the silhouettes become a layered garden.

Exploration scenario

Follow the current to document animals sheltering on a coral colony.

Research direction · proposed gameplay

Study suspension feeding and cooperative habitat structure; proposed village filtration research.

Generator constraints

  • Require hard substrate and sufficient food/current.
  • Cluster colonies; vary height, spacing and orientation.
  • Exclude the hot center of active vents.

Canyon walls

A wall fills the view. Bedding, cracks and undercuts create stacked shelters; attached life uses suitable exposed surfaces.

Reference: NOAA East of Veatch Canyon image, Exploring Atlantic Canyons and Seamounts 2014.

Explore the habitat recipe

Terrain to build

Walls, terraces, eroded pockets and talus below the cliff.

Movement and atmosphere

Cross-flow around ledges; localized sediment plumes near the bottom.

Arrival

The floor slopes, scattered blocks increase, then a wall rises into the headlamp beam.

Exploration scenario

Search successive ledges and look beneath overhangs for sheltered life.

Research direction · proposed gameplay

Generator constraints

  • Keep swim routes around ledges.

04 / Heat and chemical energy

Hydrothermal vent fields

Dark mineral towers, turbulent mineral-laden fluid and dense animal patches. Life concentrates around suitable mixed waters rather than filling every surface.

Vent crustaceansGastropodsPolychaete wormsChemosynthetic microbial communities

Reference: NOAA 2016 Deepwater Exploration of the Marianas: active chimney with vent animals.

Explore the habitat recipe

Terrain to build

Fractured volcanic substrate, chimneys and mineral aprons.

Movement and atmosphere

Rising fluid bends with the ambient current; shimmering flow and moving animals define the scene.

Arrival

Fractures and mineral staining lead to diffuse flow, then the towers appear through suspended particles.

Exploration scenario

Compare a cool mixing zone with a hotter vent outlet using instruments.

Research direction · proposed gameplay

Microbial heat/chemical adaptations; engineered bioreactor concepts remain game fiction.

Generator constraints

  • Require hydrothermal circulation in suitable geology.
  • Mix hot outlet fluid into cold surrounding water.
  • Use province-appropriate species; this Marianas image does not document Riftia.

Variations within this habitat

Isolated active tower / Dense chimney cluster / Diffuse-flow apron beside an inactive tower

Black smoke is suspended minerals, not combustion or a universal gas-bubble effect.

05 / Chemical energy without volcanic heat

Cold seeps

Mussel beds gather under a carbonate ledge, with patchy microbial cover and sediment around them. Bubble streams occur at some seeps, not all.

Seep musselsMicrobial matsSeep tubeworms at suitable sitesAssociated grazers and scavengers

Reference: NOAA Bathymodiolus mussels beneath carbonate; secondary image shows an Alaska bubbling seep.

Explore the habitat recipe

Terrain to build

Sediment, carbonate crusts, cracks and overhangs.

Movement and atmosphere

Where documented, escaping gas bubbles rise from small sources; other seeps appear visually quiet.

Arrival

An isolated shell patch leads to carbonate slabs, then a living bed beneath an overhang.

Exploration scenario

Trace a chemical gradient and compare a living mussel bed with empty shells.

Research direction · proposed gameplay

Methane- and sulfur-associated symbioses; proposed chemical-sensing technology.

Generator constraints

  • Require a localized fluid/chemical source.
  • Tie animals to gradients and attachment surfaces.
  • Keep hydrate and bubbles conditional on local conditions.

Variations within this habitat

Carbonate overhang bed / Patchy mat plain / Bubbling seep among mixed attached life

06 / A sharp chemical boundary

Brine-pool margins

Boundary-associated microbial communitiesSite-dependent mussels or other margin fauna

Reference: NOAA Gulf of Mexico, 19 April 2018: 1.2 m pool at 1,067 m depth.

Explore the habitat recipe

Terrain to build

A depression linked to saline fluid sources, with a distinct rim.

Movement and atmosphere

Density-interface ripples and refraction; avoid treating it as an air-filled lake.

Arrival

Sediment changes around a depression and a reflective-looking boundary resolves at close range.

Exploration scenario

Sample along the edge while comparing salinity and oxygen readings.

Research direction · proposed gameplay

Salt-adaptation research candidates; village applications require a documented organism.

Generator constraints

  • Require suitable salt-bearing geology and brine supply.
  • Constrain pools to depressions.
  • Do not fill anoxic brine with ordinary fish and corals.

07 / Quiet detail and scattered life

Abyssal nodule fields

Small dark nodules interrupt fine sediment. Repeated small forms create texture; trails, burrows and isolated animals provide discoveries rather than a continuous forest.

Nodule-attached organismsSediment faunaSea cucumbers and brittle stars at suitable sitesMicrobial communities

Reference: NOAA 2026 Cook Islands ROV Exploration, Dive 08: nodule-covered seamount substrate, starting depth 3,700 m.

Explore the habitat recipe

Terrain to build

Broad sediment expanses with varying nodule cover and occasional exposed rock.

Movement and atmosphere

Slow benthic activity and drifting marine snow; background density stays modest.

Arrival

Rock relief diminishes, sediment opens out and scattered nodules gradually become a field.

Exploration scenario

Use the scanner to discover small life attached to hard nodules.

Research direction · proposed gameplay

Explore attachment and slow-growth strategies; proposed gentle sampling tools.

Generator constraints

  • Keep background animal abundance lower than biological hotspots.
  • Vary nodule size and coverage with coherent patches.
  • Do not imply nodules regenerate on a gameplay timescale.

Variations within this habitat

Scattered nodules / Dense dark field / Burrowed sediment corridor

This photograph is seamount substrate, not proof of a flat plain. Not all abyssal seabeds contain nodules or share this density.

09 / Deepest-region reference

Hadal trench mosaics

The Java Trench figure shows both fine sediment and rougher slopes. The trench is a mosaic of terrain, not an endless vertical crack or a universal lush reef.

Pressure-adapted microbesSite-specific amphipods and other invertebrates

Reference: Jamieson et al. 2022, Figure 4; Java Trench observations including 5,760–7,168 m panels.

Explore the habitat recipe

Terrain to build

Fine sediment on flatter floors; bedrock and debris on steeper sections.

Movement and atmosphere

Sparse local animal movement and instrument-led discoveries.

Arrival

The route descends through progressively changing substrate and relief, with suitable depth-specific life.

Exploration scenario

Survey a sediment pocket and nearby rock exposure while monitoring pressure.

Research direction · proposed gameplay

Pressure adaptation; fictional high-pressure lab upgrades need documented species evidence.

Generator constraints

  • Reserve for a genuine deeper progression band.
  • Use pressure/depth checks independently of geology.
  • Potential chemosynthetic deposits in the study remain a hypothesis, not confirmed vents.

Variations within this habitat

Soft floor / Rocky slope / Sediment pocket below a wall

Generate variation, preserve place

A world seed creates the regional layout. Returning to the same coordinates should bring back the same geology and established discoveries. Regions blend across space; they do not reroll when you turn around. New worlds or a separately designed expedition mode can use different seeds.

Prototype pacing target: a readable clue or small discovery every 20–45 seconds of ordinary swimming, with larger destinations around 1–3 minutes apart. These are design targets to test, not scientific densities or implemented guarantees.
